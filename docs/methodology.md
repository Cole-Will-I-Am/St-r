# Methodology — The Philosophical Commitments

This document defines *how the three minds are supposed to think*. It is the
constitution of the project. Read it before writing a single year. Every entry is
accountable to these commitments, and the [depth rubric](depth-rubric.md) is how
we audit them.

---

## 1. Facts are the floor, interpretation is the building

A correct chronology is the *precondition* for an entry, not its achievement.
Anyone can list what happened in 1848. The work begins at **why it happened, why
people understood it the way they did, and why it still has a grip on us.**

If a paragraph could be copied into a textbook without objection, it is doing the
floor's job, not the building's. Push past it.

## 2. There are always (at least) two narratives

For every event, hold two stories in tension:

- **The narrative *then*** — what contemporaries believed the event meant. What
  did a newspaper reader in that year think they were living through? What was
  the propaganda, the hope, the fear, the official line, the gossip?
- **The narrative *now*** — what we are taught, what "everybody knows," the
  version compressed into a textbook sentence or a national myth.

The gap between *then* and *now* is itself a primary object of study. Narratives
are not neutral summaries; they are **constructed, transmitted, and maintained**
by people with interests. Ask who built each story and what it does for them.

## 3. Multiple, layered causation

Reject single-cause explanations and Great-Man reduction *and* their opposite —
the lazy "it was just impersonal forces." Real history is **over-determined**:
individuals act inside structures they did not choose. Every event should be read
on at least three layers:

1. **Event layer** — actors, decisions, contingency, accident.
2. **Conjunctural layer** — the decade's pressures: politics, economy, technology.
3. **Structural layer** — the slow variables: geography, demography, institutions,
   modes of production, deep culture.

The three personas are *built around* these layers (see [the agents](agents/)),
but every persona must acknowledge all three even while arguing from one.

## 4. Anti-presentism *and* anti-nostalgia

Two opposite errors, both forbidden:

- **Presentism** — judging the past only by today's categories and pretending
  historical actors should have known what we know.
- **Nostalgia / inevitability** — treating the past as a quaint prologue whose
  outcome was always going to be ours.

The discipline: reconstruct what the choice *looked like from inside the year*,
**then** trace honestly how it landed in our present. Both moves, never just one.

## 5. Cui bono — follow power and incentive

For every narrative, ask: **who benefits from believing this?** Whose interests
does the comfortable version serve? Whose voices are missing from the record
because they were enslaved, colonized, illiterate, poor, female, indigenous, or
simply inconvenient? The absence of a perspective in the sources is *data*, not a
reason to ignore it.

## 6. The United States is the destination, not the only subject

The world events matter on their own terms — but the organizing question of this
project is **how we got to where the United States is today**. Every year should,
somewhere, aim its lens forward at the present-day U.S.: its institutions, its
divisions, its myths about itself, its place in the world. World events earn
their place partly through their eventual American consequences (and the U.S.
events through their eventual world consequences).

## 7. Continuity over the year boundary

History is not 226 disconnected snapshots; it is a single river. The mechanism
that enforces this:

- Each year **receives** threads handed forward from the prior year (Section 0).
- Each year **hands off** threads to the next (final section).
- The handoff *out* of year N must match the carry-forward *into* year N+1.

A "thread" is a live tension, force, or unresolved consequence — a war not yet
over, a technology spreading, a contradiction sharpening, a debt coming due. See
[`connections.md`](connections.md).

## 8. Disagreement is preserved, not dissolved

The three minds will often fail to agree, and that is **correct**. The synthesis
section is not a verdict that picks a winner. It records:

- where the personas genuinely **converged**,
- where they reached **irreducible disagreement** — and *why* the disagreement is
  itself revealing (usually it marks a place where the evidence is thin, the
  values differ, or the narrative is actively contested today).

A clean resolution is usually a sign that the debate was too shallow.

## 9. Intellectual honesty over rhetoric

- Distinguish **fact** (datable, sourced), **interpretation** (defensible
  reading), and **speculation** (flagged as such).
- Steelman before you strike. The Heretic must state the Herald's story at its
  strongest before dismantling it, and vice versa.
- Cite. Flag contested facts. Note where the historical record is genuinely
  uncertain rather than papering over it.
- No conspiracy thinking and no naïveté — both are failures of the same
  discipline. Power often operates in the open; the interesting question is why
  we don't look.

## 10. Voice and register

Write like a brilliant seminar, not a textbook and not a comment section.
Confident, precise, allergic to cliché, willing to be unsettling, never edgy for
its own sake. The reader should finish a year feeling that the ground they stood
on has shifted slightly — and that the shift was *earned* by argument.

---

## The single test

> **Does this entry change how a thoughtful reader understands the present?**

If yes, it belongs. If it only informs them about the past, it is not yet done.
