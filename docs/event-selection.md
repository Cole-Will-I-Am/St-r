# Event Selection — Choosing the Six

Each year gets **exactly six events: 3 United States + 3 world**. Choosing *which
six* is itself an interpretive act, so it is done deliberately and the reasoning
is logged.

---

## The quota

- **3 U.S. events** — occurring within the United States or primarily about it.
- **3 world events** — occurring outside the U.S. (an event can be "world" even if
  it later shaped America; classify by where it happened / whom it primarily
  concerned).

For the years before there was much of a "United States" to speak of (early
1800s), U.S. events may be small in world-historical terms but large in the
national through-line — that is fine and intended. The point is the *trajectory*.

---

## Selection criteria (weigh, don't checklist)

Choose events that maximize **interpretive yield**, not just fame:

1. **Consequence** — did it bend the trajectory, then or in hindsight?
2. **Narrative friction** — is there a gap between the story then, the story now,
   and the reality? High-friction events are gold.
3. **Connective power** — does it link to the year's other events or to the
   running through-line?
4. **Through-line relevance** — does a thread from it reach the present-day U.S.?
5. **Counter-canonical value** — sometimes the *un*famous event (a court ruling, a
   patent, a migration, a price collapse) explains more than the famous one.
   Don't auto-pick the obvious headline if a quieter event yields more.

A deliberately mixed slate is encouraged: not three wars, but perhaps a war, an
economic shift, and a cultural/technological turn.

---

## Balance across a year's six

Aim for variety of *kind* across the six so the year isn't monochrome:

- political / legal
- economic / technological
- social / cultural / demographic
- military / geopolitical

Not every category every year — but if all six are battles, reconsider.

---

## Logging the choice

In the year file, briefly note **why these six and not others** — especially any
deliberately *non-obvious* pick, and any famous event you chose to *omit* and why.
Disagreement among the personas about the slate is recorded, not hidden.

---

## Anachronism guard

Label events by what they were *to contemporaries*, then separately by what they
became. A treaty barely noticed in its year may be pivotal in retrospect — say
both. Don't retroactively inflate an event's contemporary importance to justify
its selection; its retrospective importance is reason enough.
